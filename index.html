A partitioner you can read in an afternoon.

hMETIS, the most influential partitioner, is closed source, and KaHyPar, the leading open framework, spans about 27,000 lines of C++. New ideas therefore tend to wrap hMETIS as a black box or replace a single stage of KaHyPar. EvoPart is a complete multilevel partitioner in one self-contained, single-threaded file.

A harness for evolving a whole solver.

Built on OpenEvolve. The harness changes how programs enter and stay in the population, and feeds every program's measured cut and wall time back into the prompt that edits it next.

  1. Sample from the archive

    Four islands of MAP-Elites grids hold up to 150 programs, with migration every 25 generations.

    1Cells are indexed by behavior, not text: speed = log2 of the slowdown against KaHyPar, and k-profile = q3/√(q2q4).

  2. Build the prompt

    The task, training grid and scoring rules; the parent with its description and its last measurements; three inspirations from the parent's island.

  3. Write a child

    Claude Opus 5 (weight 0.8) or Claude Fable 5.1 (weight 0.2) replies with diffs. No tools, and no view of the partitioners installed on the machine.

  4. Evaluate in a cascade

    1 cell, then 5 cells with one seed, then all 30 cells with three seeds. Cut and balance are recomputed from the partition files the program writes.

    2A child whose 90 cuts hash to the same fingerprint as a resident of its island keeps only the higher-scoring of the two.

The score, the cut and wall time of every cell, and whatever the program printed are stored with the child, and appear in the prompt whenever it is chosen as a parent. Programs can therefore build their own diagnostics for their descendants.

Score

score= (∏i,k crefi,k / ci,k)1/Ncut quality− clamp(0.02·log2τ, ±0.14)time term− min(10−8·chars, 0.003)code size

cref is KaHyPar's median cut over three seeds on cell (i, k); τ is the geometric mean slowdown against KaHyPar. A 2× slowdown costs as much as 2% of cut quality. A cell that fails to compile, crashes, times out or violates the balance constraint scores zero, which zeroes the whole score.

What the model wrote.

A multilevel recursive-bisection partitioner with the hypergraph in CSR arrays. Most of it follows established practice in hMETIS, KaHyPar and PaToH. Two components are, to our knowledge, not part of existing hypergraph partitioners.

  1. 1

    Coarsening

    Heavy-edge clustering by net-size class down to about 200 vertices; identical coarse nets are merged.

    New component ❶ lives here
  2. 2

    Initial partitioning

    3 to 30 greedy growths from random seeds on the coarsest graph, each polished by FM; the best is kept.

  3. 3

    Refinement

    Boundary FM at every level, whole-net compound moves on the finer levels, and flow refinement with Dinic's algorithm at the finest level, alternated with FM until the cut stops improving.

  4. 4

    Restarts and V-cycles

    3 to 24 restarts per bisection with early stopping, then flow, up to 8 V-cycles, and flow again.

  5. 5

    k parts

    Recursive bisection that reserves 30% of the balance slack for the bisections below; run twice for k ≥ 3.

    New component ❷ lives here
1New component

Racing two coarsenings with opposite net-size priorities

Heavy-edge rating barely sees large nets, and single-vertex FM cannot repair them once they are split. When nets with more than 60 pins hold at least 12% of all pins, the restarts of every bisection alternate between two coarsenings and keep the better bisection. Rather than deciding per instance which nets to merge first, EvoPart tries both.

On the 21 Titan23 instances where it triggers, racing gives a lower cut than either coarsening alone: using only heavy-edge clustering raises the cut by 3%, using only whole-net contraction raises it by 14%. It is no slower than either. See ablations

Even restarts: heavy-edge clustering, small nets first

Odd restarts: contract whole nets of 8 to 512 pins, largest first

Both run inside the restarts; the better bisection is kept.
2New component

Re-partitioning pairs of final blocks

Recursive bisection commits to its top split before the blocks below it exist. After the k blocks are formed, EvoPart merges each pair of blocks, bisects the union again, and keeps the new split whenever it lowers the cut. One pass over all pairs is a sweep; up to three sweeps run while each still lowers the cut.

At k = 3 and 4, the sweeps lower the cut by 4–9% on Titan23 and 1.5–1.9% on ISPD98, at 1.8× and 1.3× the running time. Under the score, that trade is worth making. See ablations

One sweep visits all k(k − 1)/2 pairs of blocks; with k = 4 that is six pairs.

Lower cut than KaHyPar and hMETIS.

Over all 40 instances, EvoPart's cut is 5.5% lower than KaHyPar's and 5.1% lower than hMETIS's, in 70% of KaHyPar's wall time and about the same wall time as hMETIS.
